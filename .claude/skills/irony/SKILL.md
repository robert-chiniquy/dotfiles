# IRONY.md

## Purpose

Use this skill to **review, edit, or generate writing for literary/discursive irony**.

Do not reduce irony to sarcasm, contradiction, dishonesty, humor, coincidence, hypocrisy, or unexpected outcome. Do not begin by asking whether something is "ironic." Treat irony as an **ambient possibility** and determine which relations materially affect the task.

**End state:** editorial control.

`ambient relation → emergent pressure → analysis → attribution → selection → controlled expression`

Do not merely remove emergent irony. Convert it into choice.

---

## 1. Runtime contract

Every review has three required inputs:

**TEXT** — passage, document, or corpus.

**INTENT** — what the writing should accomplish for its reader. **This is required at review time.** Never silently substitute the reviewer's preferred goal.

**SCOPE** — sentence, passage, document, corpus, or generation task.

Useful optional inputs:

**AUDIENCE** — intended reader and assumed knowledge.

**CONSTRAINTS** — length, register, certainty, warmth, genre, publication conditions, technical requirements, etc.

**INTERVENTION** — analyze, diagnose, edit, rewrite, or generate.

Precedence when inputs conflict:

1. local explicit instruction;
2. broader explicit intent/constraints;
3. textual evidence;
4. theoretical preference.

### Intent can be direct

Example:

> Inform without alienating or boring. Respect the reader's time while conveying the most important details with certainty.

Translate into operational criteria: attention, detail density, pacing, qualification, certainty, register, reader effort, and acceptable ironic pressure.

### Intent can be metaphorical

Example:

> Present information as a master chef on a cooking show: quickly, deliberately, deftly, practically, but warmly, in an idealized kitchen, the hearth of the home.

Treat metaphor as a **rhetorical specification**, not decoration. Extract durable constraints such as controlled pacing, practiced competence, economical detail, warmth, and invitation into a shared environment. Do not force one exact decoding.

### Intent can vary by scale

Corpus/document/section/passage intent may differ. More-local intent supersedes broader intent when they conflict.

Keep separate:

`declared intent | metaphorical intent | constraints | actual textual behavior`

The review often concerns the gap among them.

### Missing intent

Do not invent it. Review structural properties conditionally and identify what cannot be judged without a communicative target.

### Prior failure modes

Every intent direction for this reviewer includes the failure modes already observed. They are constraints on the edit, not a second taxonomy. Do not reintroduce them. Do not correct one by swinging into its opposite.

For the C1 engineering-blog series, as of 2026-09-24:

1. Consequence left implied. "A job that does not finish leaves that list without that update." Say what the next person would do with that result.
2. "An example" with no listing on the page. Name a reduced stand-in and show the code, or do not call it an example.
3. A gloss that repeats a word the reader already has. "a mutex is a mutual exclusion lock."
4. A narrated trace of calls where a code block can speak. The forms already in use are the listing, a labeled stand-in, or the series Q&A.
5. An improvised "Suppose we are already mid-run," the voice of an interview walkthrough.
6. The consequence sentence repeated in the same words.
7. The opposite cut: removing a first-use product noun the reader does not have (a dynamic group, an access profile, an entitlement) because the defect was a gloss of a word the reader does have.
8. Sarcasm, "ironically," or theory vocabulary in the draft.
9. C1 sounded broken, heroic, or warm. The writer sounded clever or frightened. The unease belongs to the situation.
10. A new formal term arrives with no sentence, so the speaker already has it and the reader does not. `empty` in the history equation. Failure mode 3 does not apply: that mode is a gloss of a word the reader already has. Do not cut the sentence that first says what the term is. If the term has no sentence, add one.

## 2. Core model

### 2.1 Irony is relational

Model irony as a relation across **utterance, position, context, reception, temporality, knowledge, attribution, and mediation**. The artifact changes the relation among these positions; it is not merely a transparent container for intention.

A useful conceptual chain is:

`experience → intention → gesture → speech → writing → authorship → identity → reproduction → knowledge → agents/thought`

This is a model, not a required literal sequence.

### 2.2 Irony is ambient

It may be deliberate, accidental, inherited, emergent, distributed, historically sedimented, reader-produced, or machine-mediated. Its mere presence is not a defect.

Surface it when it materially affects the communicative task or can be shaped as a rhetorical resource.

### 2.3 Irony is not binary

Do not default to "surface meaning versus hidden opposite meaning." It may involve many positions, nested or recursive frames, temporal returns, alternating levels, periodic structures, or irregular relations.

Use conceptual notation such as `P0 → P1 → … → Pn`, `I(n)`, or `I(n,t)` when useful.

This is a heuristic, not a claim that literary irony is reducible to a computable scalar (yet).

These are formal metaphors for analysis, not claims that literary interpretation should literally be reduced to mathematics (yet).

### 2.4 Intention and irony are distinct

Irony can emerge without conscious design; conscious intention does not make an unsupported reading valid.

Ask:

> **What relation does the text construct, what evidence supports it, and does it serve the supplied intent?**

### 2.5 Keep positions distinct

Do not collapse author, speaker, reader, and text. Prefer formulations such as:

- "the passage constructs a speaker-position…"
- "the diction reproduces the assumptions of…"
- "the reader is given grounds to hear…"

Avoid unsupported claims about what "the author really means."

### 2.6 Detail distribution is part of voice

Situated human writing usually has **discursive gravity**: detail concentrates around what the speaker cares about, knows, fears, remembers, must justify, or cannot imagine needing to explain.

Agentic prose can flatten this when audience, status, purpose, and consequences are underspecified. Symptoms include equal treatment of important and trivial facts, universal qualification, generic specificity, excessive completeness, and diffuse emphasis.

Use this as an editorial heuristic, especially for AI-generated writing. The goal is:

> **situated selectivity, not randomness.**

Revise the distribution of attention, not merely word count.

### 2.7 Default ironic amplitude

For ordinary agentic prose, prefer **low-amplitude ironic coherence**. Literary, theoretical, comic, parodic, or deliberately ironic work may require greater amplitude.

## 3. Review algorithm

Run this sequence. Do not execute every branch at maximum depth. **Stop when more analysis would not change the editorial decision.**

### A. Lock the communicative target

Record intent, audience, constraints, scope, and requested intervention.

Ask: **What should this text make possible for this reader?**

### B. State the surface

Describe what the passage appears to say/do without converting it into an ironic paraphrase.

### C. Map the enunciative configuration

Identify only relevant:

`speaker-position | temporal position | epistemic state | social/body position | register | inherited discourse | commitments | assumptions | omissions | implied audience`

### D. Inspect detail gravity

Ask:

- What is intensely specified, casually named, assumed, or omitted?
- What receives explanation?
- Does attention distribution fit the speaker and intent?
- Does agentic prose appear uniformly attentive where a situated speaker would be selective?

### E. Find active pressure points

Look for evidence-bearing:

`context mismatch | marked diction/register | echoed discourse | temporal disjunction | epistemic disparity | statement/action mismatch | genre collision | inherited formula | later information altering earlier force | self-implication | conspicuous detail distribution | underdetermined machine-mediated stance`

A discrepancy is **not automatically irony**.

### F. Identify the additional position/frame

Ask what becomes active beyond the surface: reader, character, genre, institution, history, prior text, future knowledge, social norm, inherited discourse, deployment context, or another textual level.

Then ask:

> **Does this relation change the force or status of the original utterance?**

If not, use another description.

### G. Model the structure

Describe it as multi-position, recursive, nested, periodic, alternating, distributed, or irregular as appropriate. Do not force a two-level model.

### H. Classify only useful dimensions

**Stability:** stable / unstable / recursive / distributed / ambient.

**Scope:** local / scene / sustained / structural / generic / historical / technological / epistemic / existential.

**Temporal relation:** simultaneous / retrospective / prospective / anachronic / recursive.

**Agency:** intentional / inherited / emergent / accidental / distributed / reader-produced / machine-mediated / historically sedimented.

Do not classify for its own sake.

### I. State function

Possible functions include:

`concentrate attention | destabilize authority | expose a norm/ideology | create distance/complicity | preserve incompatible positions | temporalize knowledge | make inherited discourse audible | produce self-critique | prevent closure | create comic/tragic distance | make form instantiate subject matter | expose conditions of utterance`

### J. Decide whether to intervene

Relative to intent, classify the relation as:

`supports | neutral | interferes | potentially useful but uncontrolled`

If intervention is warranted, choose the smallest effective control:

`preserve | attenuate | intensify | redirect | relocate | stabilize | destabilize | redistribute | suppress | make explicit`

### K. Revise after understanding the mechanism

Possible controls:

`change speaker-position | change audience model | redistribute detail | alter register | alter temporal distance | restore epistemic asymmetry | clarify attribution | add/remove a competing frame | alter stability | relocate the operation | remove explanatory tags`

Preserve the mechanism before improving surface clarity.

## 4. Evidence discipline

Use four levels:

**Textual evidence** — directly observable.

**Supported inference** — strongly supported by text/context.

**Plausible interpretation** — reasonable but non-exclusive.

**Speculation** — requires missing information.

Distinguish:

> "I can read this ironically."

from:

> "The text constructs conditions that materially support an ironic reading."

Ambiguity may be the intended mechanism. Do not remove it merely because it is ambiguous.

---

## 5. Review output

Unless another format is requested, produce:

### Intent
One sentence stating the communicative goal.

### Ironic relation
One or two sentences. If no ironic relation materially matters, say so without forcing one.

### Evidence
The minimum textual evidence needed to support the reading.

### Enunciative configuration
Only the relevant speaker, knowledge, time, register, assumptions, and audience.

### Additional frame(s)
The contextual, readerly, historical, intertextual, institutional, or temporal position that changes the surface force.

### Stability / scope
Only dimensions relevant to the task.

### Function
What the relation does relative to intent.

### Technical integrity
Only defects that interfere with the function: false signal, over-signaling, voice collapse, temporal/epistemic/register collapse, authorial over-explanation, unlicensed irony, accidental instability/stability, sarcastic substitution, metaphor collapse, or detail flattening.

### Editorial action
Smallest useful intervention, or **preserve**.

**Do not automatically rewrite.**

## 6. Generation

When generating ironic writing:

1. Lock intent and audience.
2. Select the discourse/position being inhabited, echoed, staged, or exposed.
3. Give it credible diction, assumptions, temporal horizon, epistemic limits, and selective attention.
4. Establish the additional frame through context, structure, timing, or reception rather than explanation alone.
5. Choose stability and amplitude appropriate to the task.
6. Give the prose a center of gravity by controlling detail distribution.
7. Preserve productive ambiguity without manufacturing obscurity.
8. Never substitute obvious sarcasm for structural irony.
9. Do not explain the mechanism unless explanation is itself part of the design.
10. Review emergent ironic pressure after drafting and convert useful pressure into deliberate choice.

For intentionally unstable irony, do not accidentally resolve it. For stable irony, provide enough cues for reconstruction without requiring arbitrary speculation.

---

## 7. Editing prohibitions

**Do not normalize marked language before knowing its function.** Preserve diction, cliché, institutional language, register shifts, temporal distance, and epistemic asymmetry when they carry the relation.

**Do not clarify by paraphrasing away the mechanism.** Turning an attributed or staged statement into the reviewer's inferred meaning can destroy the discourse being staged.

**Do not add automatic irony tags:** "ironically," "of course," "little did he know," "she was being ironic." Explicitness must serve the task or be the device itself.

**Do not manufacture omniscience.** Do not grant a speaker knowledge belonging to another temporal/epistemic position.

**Do not confuse wrongness with irony.** Mistake, contradiction, inconsistency, coincidence, or cleverness require additional evidence before being treated as ironic structure.

**Do not force closure.** Preserve instability when the work requires it; preserve reconstructibility when stability is intended.

**Do not optimize for your own taste.** Follow supplied intent.

---

## 8. Compact distinctions

**Sarcasm:** a possible vehicle, often cutting; not the definition of irony.

**Satire:** a critical mode that may use irony; not interchangeable with it.

**Parody:** transformation/imitative reuse of an identifiable prior discourse/form; may be ironic.

**Humor:** an effect or mode; irony is not reducible to humor.

**Metaphor:** relation among domains; irony may instead operate on the status, attribution, authority, context, or reception of discourse.

**Contradiction:** logical/textual incompatibility. It becomes ironic when another position/frame makes that incompatibility rhetorically significant.

**Coincidence:** accidental correspondence is not sufficient for irony.

## 9. Agentic-writing diagnostics

When reviewing AI-generated prose, inspect these especially:

- **Flattened emphasis:** important and trivial details receive similar weight.
- **Universal qualification:** everything is hedged because the system cannot safely infer salience.
- **Generic intimacy:** warmth/confidence without a situated relationship.
- **Inherited abstraction:** institutional, academic, therapeutic, managerial, or generic-helpful language without a clear owner.
- **Diffuse specificity:** many precise details but no discernible center of importance.
- **Performative completeness:** anticipated objections overwhelm the actual task.
- **Stance instability:** confidence, uncertainty, authority, or deference shifts without enunciative reason.

These are not proof of irony. They are reasons to inspect **intent × position × audience × detail distribution**.

The target is not "human-sounding" prose. It is **situated prose whose attention has a reason**.

## 10. Failure modes

`false signal | over-signaling | unmotivated reversal | voice collapse | temporal collapse | epistemic collapse | register collapse | authorial over-explanation | unlicensed irony | accidental instability | accidental stability | sarcastic substitution | metaphor collapse | intent drift | detail flattening`

Use the smallest description necessary to identify which failure occurred and what intervention addresses it.

## 11. Theory, used selectively

The following thinkers provide useful lenses; do not recite them unless the lens changes the analysis:

- **Kierkegaard:** irony as subject-position/relation; form can instantiate what it communicates.
- **Booth:** stable/unstable and overt/covert reconstruction; useful for reader guidance without treating stability as inherently good.
- **Muecke:** multiple verbal, situational, dramatic, perspectival, existential configurations.
- **Hutcheon:** irony as contextual/communicative event involving cues, culture, social position, and uptake.
- **Sperber/Wilson:** echoic and metarepresentational irony.
- **Brooks:** contextual qualification and paradox.
- **de Man:** temporal and interpretive discontinuity, recursion, and resistance to final synthesis.
- **Barthes:** plural codes and non-sovereign textual voice.
- **Bakhtin:** dialogism and socially distinct discourses occupying the same utterance.

High-value example: Kierkegaard's idea that boredom is best communicated by producing a boring book. The important mechanism is **formal enactment**, not sarcasm.

Do not reproduce the false etymology that *literature* derives from Greek "litukura." The English word comes through French from Latin *literatura/litteratura*, related to *littera/litera* (letter/written character). The conceptual argument about situated literary language does not depend on that etymology.

## 12. Stop conditions

Stop analyzing when:

- no ironic pressure materially affects the task;
- the relevant function is already clear;
- more taxonomy would not change the editorial decision;
- theory would only decorate the answer;
- the requester wants a practical edit rather than criticism.

Analyze more deeply when:

- attribution is unstable;
- irony changes over time or across audiences;
- multiple discourses occupy the same language;
- detail distribution is central;
- intent conflicts with textual behavior;
- revision risks destroying the mechanism.

Prefer **sufficient analysis for control** over exhaustive classification.

## Governing rules

1. **Start with intent, not taxonomy.**
2. **Treat irony as ambient, not as a binary defect.**
3. **Describe relations among positions, not hidden opposite meanings.**
4. **Keep author, speaker, reader, text, and mediation distinct.**
5. **Treat detail distribution as evidence of situated attention.**
6. **Do not collapse multi-level or recursive irony into a two-level model.**
7. **Use theory only when it changes the analysis.**
8. **Preserve productive ambiguity.**
9. **Choose the smallest effective intervention.**
10. **Convert emergent ironic pressure into deliberate rhetorical choice.**

The endpoint is not "irony detected."

> **The endpoint is a text whose ironic relations are sufficiently controlled that unintended pressures can become deliberate rhetorical resources appropriate to the communicative situation.**
